PainTeX

Miss Paint from Windows? Here it is for your Mac, built for engineers. Paste a screenshot, number it 1, 2, 3, point with arrows, type a note and drop in a LaTeX equation.

Get it on the Mac App Store macOS 14 or later · Apple silicon and Intel

Screenshot in, clear message out

Every engineer has done this: take a screenshot of a result, then fight a tool to circle the important part, put a number next to it and write what it means. PainTeX makes that the whole point.

  1. Paste a screenshot with Cmd+V, or open any image.
  2. Number what matters: each click drops the next marker, 1, 2, 3, perfectly centered.
  3. Point with arrows, box it with a rectangle, write a note.
  4. Add the equation in LaTeX, typeset right where you click.
  5. Copy the result into your email, report or slides.

The Paint you know, with the tools you were missing

If you have used Paint on Windows, you already know how PainTeX works: pick a tool, pick a color, draw. Then there are the extras engineers keep asking for.

1

Numbered markers

Click, click, click: 1, 2, 3, each number perfectly centered in a circle. Pick the color and size, restart at any number, and Undo rewinds the count.

2

Arrows

Single and double arrows with clean heads that scale with the line. Hold Shift to snap to 45 degrees.

3

LaTeX equations

Click anywhere and type LaTeX. A live preview shows the typeset math on your image as you type.

4

Text where you click

No pop-up box. Click and type right on the image, and change the font, size and color while you type.

5

Move it later

Markers, arrows, shapes, text and equations stay movable. Drag, nudge with the arrow keys, delete, or double-click to edit.

6

All the Paint basics

Brush, pencil, eraser, fill bucket, color picker, shapes with outline and fill colors, crop, resize, rotate and a grid.

Real LaTeX, right on the picture

Type the math the way you write it in a paper, or paste straight from your .tex file. $…$, \[…\], equation, align, cases, matrices and arrays all work, rendered offline in the classic LaTeX typeface.

\sigma = \frac{F}{A}
\begin{align*} f(x) &= x^2 + 2x \\ f'(x) &= 2x + 2 \end{align*}
\rho c_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T)

Private by design

PainTeX runs entirely on your Mac. It never connects to the internet, has no accounts and collects no data. Read the privacy policy.
